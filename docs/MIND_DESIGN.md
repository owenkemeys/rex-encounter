# Mind design note — danger by default, beliefs, and general senses (2026-11-14, rev 2)

Status: agreed direction from discussions with the owner; not yet built. `claude/HANDOFF.md` says what exists. This note says what to build next in the mind and why. The owner does not want to choose between the options below: build them as one coherent design, using judgement.

## What it is for

The animal is a platform: an intelligent creature that can inhabit any space and game design and produce interesting outcomes there. Everything below must be a general mechanism driven by properties of things and events, never by names, and never special to the player or to our arena.

## Owner's observations this responds to

- "I am not feeling woefully exposed to a gigantic predator that could kill me at a moment's notice unless I do some serious clowning around, which breaks immersion another way." Watching it hunt prey animals is "interesting like a nature doc, but boring".
- The best moments so far: it sizing him up at a distance with its head cocking; coming up and sniffing or nudging him; the flare (it senses heat, flinches back, then decides to stamp on it). He believes the flare is fairly scripted and wants those senses, decisions and actions generalised.
- If you are food, a giant rex should pursue you aggressively. But the working-you-out is the novel part.
- It tires too quickly, so running away is never a problem.
- He has been entertained when it remembered what he did (wary of rocks, of the torch). But seeing that thought prompts the player to do exactly that; it gets annoyed, which is funny but trivialises it. "Its thoughts prompt the player (unbeknownst to it)": make that fun or intimidating whichever way the player goes.
- Theories that put you in conceptual boxes which you can subvert and it must revisit: good.
- It should NOT remember you after it kills you (a builder may choose otherwise).
- "Taste decides and the taste is fatal" is bad: either you die whenever it decides to taste, or being tasted is not perilous. But predators feeling things with their mouths, and that being extremely damaging, is good.
- The Nim Chimpsky quality ("give orange me give eat orange me eat orange…"): complex yet basic. He has felt that joy here, for example when it throws you, sees you are not moving, and declares that good.

## The core loop: you are in danger by default, and you manage it

1. **Food from the start, unhurried.** A small animal alone in the open is food. It knows it, and it is not worried about you escaping, because it is right not to be: a chase in the open ends in a catch (the chase and stamina must make this true; cover its body cannot pass, a strong distraction or a big head start are what save you). The unconcern is the dread.
2. **It comes to you.** Early in an encounter it closes the distance and inspects you at arm's length: head down at your level, sniffing, breathing, head cocked. First close inspection within roughly the first 20 seconds of noticing you, when nothing else holds it.
3. **Curiosity keeps you alive.** You are food it has not got round to eating because you are strange. New things you do buy time; repeated things wear out (habituation per kind of act). When it runs out of questions, it eats. The skill is staying interesting without looking like prey: darting, bolting and hide-and-peek still trip the prey reflex.
4. **It asks with its body.** At close range it probes, escalating: sniff, nudge, a shove that knocks you over (no injury), a lifted foot over you, an open-mouth reach. Each probe is a question; your reaction is the answer and changes its beliefs (flinch and run: prey; hold still: odd, or dead; hurt it: something that bites).
5. **A hunger clock.** It starts nearer fed-and-curious and grows hungrier over minutes, readable in thought and body. Feeding it something else winds the clock back. Hungry: it hunts what it has boxed as food. Fed: it toys (herding, blocking exits, false charges).
6. **The mouth is its hand.** It explores the world with its mouth, on everything: thrown objects, crates, a vehicle roof, a carcass, with visible catastrophic force (crush, shake, toss, tear). The player learns what mouthing does by watching it done to other things. You are on its list. Resolving the owner's dilemma:
   - If the jaws close on you, you die at once (peril model: sudden, loud, instant; no lingering injury; never a catch-and-release).
   - But the mouth is never a coin flip or a hidden verdict. The reach is slow, open and telegraphed for seconds, and it is counterable: put something else in its way or in its mouth (meat, a flare: hot, so it flinches; an object), break its line with a gap its head cannot enter, give it a stronger thing to attend to, or have lowered its interest before it got that far. The decision point is the approach, not the bite.
   - The mouth can also take things FROM you without touching you: the torch, a pack, the thing you are holding, the cover you are behind. Cost without injury: it disarms and exposes you, and you watch what happens to the object.
7. **Expectations with teeth.** Each belief carries an expectation and a prepared response. If you do what it expects, it is ready and that is its cue to act (braced for a rock, the wind-up triggers its move), not to be annoyed. If you do the opposite, its model breaks: a visible stop, a re-think, a re-filing. Its visible thoughts therefore offer the player two consequential branches; neither is free.

## Mechanisms (general)

- **Beliefs.** About any creature or thing it attends to, a few beliefs formed from what it has seen that thing do or be: throws, shines light, gives food, flees, hides, hurts, makes noise, comes back, is hot, is hard, is edible. One or two observations suffice: it is a simple mind and holds confident wrong theories.
- **Boxes are summaries.** A box (food, threat, provider, pest, toy, rival, nothing) is the current summary of its beliefs about a thing. Subverting one belief can move the thing between boxes; the move is a shown beat in thought and body.
- **Fast verdict, hard action; thinking resumes on surprise.** Sizing-up takes seconds, then it acts with commitment. A violated expectation re-opens the question.
- **Intent before action.** Plans are shown as thought and body language before it acts; the terror sits in the gap.
- **General senses, reflexes and actions.** Things carry physical properties the world supplies or the animal infers: hot, bright, loud, small or large against itself, moving, hard, soft, edible, on the ground, fits in the mouth, underfoot, held by something. Senses report them (heat near skin, glare in an eye, pain, smell). Reflexes answer them (flinch from heat or pain, turn from glare, startle). A small repertoire applies to anything whose properties allow it: sniff, nudge, shove, stamp on (small, on the ground, within a step), mouth, pick up and drop, shake, carry, avoid, guard. The flare sequence must fall out of "hot, bright, small, on the ground". Audit how the flare is done today, rebuild it on these parts, and prove the same parts give new behaviour with an object from another world (a hot ember, a bright lamp, a noisy device).
- **Builder interface.** The world feeds generic events and properties; a builder can read beliefs, box, hunger and the thought stream, and can add an event kind or a property without touching the mind.

## Voice: poor language, rich situation

The charm is the Nim Chimpsky quality. Keep its language deliberately poor while its situation gets richer; beliefs, plans and theories must all come out through the same few blunt words.
- Total earnestness: wants and verdicts stated with no self-awareness.
- Flat verdicts on grim outcomes (the "not moving. good" kind): simple and sincere, so funny and chilling together.
- Insistent repetition and re-ordering of a want as pressure builds.
- Still 2 to 5 plain lower-case words, each traceable to real state; thoughts and body tell one story. No numbers, no abstract words, no placeholders shown raw.

## Entertainment patterns to check the mechanism against

Short cause, thought, consequence arcs; none scripted, all reachable from the rules.
- Confident wrong theory: it sees you produce meat, concludes you make meat, follows expectantly, loses patience.
- You read its plan: it waits for you to look away; look away on purpose, or refuse to.
- Insulting category: filed as food for later; it leaves and comes back to check.
- It learns the trick: a lure works once; the second time it comes for you.
- Peekaboo: gone; a pause; not gone.
- Possession: fed, you become its thing: followed, guarded, not allowed to leave.
- Startle then temper: frightened by something small, angrier for it.
- It takes your torch and you watch what its mouth does to it.

## First scenario to build on top (after the core loop works in the sandbox)

"Keep it off the kids" (the paddock scene; Alan Grant's position). The player is constrained: some freedom of movement but not total, vulnerable. Others who cannot defend themselves are within its reach. Help arrives after a time. The player must keep its attention, which the general rules already make dangerous: attention is only pulled by becoming the more compelling target. If it kills the player, the camera comes to rest on the ground framed so that both the animal and the kids are visible, and it goes straight for them without stopping to eat the player: the scenario is failed because it can now reach the kids, not because the player died. Constrained movement also removes clowning and sprinting away.
